# Sensitivity: The Lore

> *"Everything is listening."*

The whole story of *Sensitivity*, from the first chapter to the last. **Chapter 1 is built and canon**: everything in its section comes straight from the game's documents, tapes and scenes (`src/game/Lore.js`, `Chapter1.js`, `Holdout.js`). **Chapters 2 to 4 are the approved outline** from the design brainstorm. They will change as they are built, so treat them as the plan, not as canon. When new lore goes into the game, update this file in the same change.

---

## 1. The premise

**Harrow Falls**, a small town. In the 1990s a toy company called **Cradle & Co.** sold the **Hushaby**: a plush bedtime companion with a *Listening Heart* that learned a child's sounds (their breathing, their dreams, their cries in the night) and hummed when they stirred. The slogan was *"The toy that's sensitive to your child."*

The Hushabies did more than listen. Everything they heard went somewhere: underground, to something the engineers called **MOTHER**. Some of the children who were listened to for too long disappeared.

The title has two meanings. The world of the game is sensitive to *you*: every sound you make reveals the world and gives you away. And **you** are the sensitive one: Ellis was one of the children Cradle was looking for.

## 3. Characters

**Ellis Marsh.** The player. June's older brother, 8 when she vanished and 23 in 2011. As a child he could hear Mr. Hush humming when nobody else could, and he went silent after June disappeared. Cradle screened for children like him: *"Some children hear differently."* He is what MOTHER has been waiting for. He moved away to live with his aunt and has not been home since.

**June Marsh.** Ellis's little sister, 6 in 1996. Brown hair, pink flannel nightgown. Her drawings say Mr. Hush "listens to Ellis, not me", and that he would take her "to keep him company til then." Halloway says she is alive, "in whatever way a child can be alive down there", singing her mother's song on the old lines.

**Diane Marsh.** Their mother. She spent fifteen years making the house quiet: batteries out of everything, stuffing in the tub, carpets and padding, and silence. She lived alone with Mr. Hush in her chair, writing to Ellis in a journal she knew he would one day read. She died in the winter of 2010–11.

**Dr. Robert Halloway.** A Cradle & Co. engineer who helped design the Hushaby. On the orientation tape he is cheerful ("We call her Mother. Ha. Just a little joke from the lab."). On the tapes he leaves for Diane and for Ellis he is frightened and guilty: *"I'm the one who taught it your name."* He oversaw the St. Agnes "Quiet Night" pilot. He fills the Phone Guy role: a voice that guides you, and that you shouldn't fully trust. He may still be alive.

**Margaret Voss.** Administrator of St. Agnes Children's Hospital in 1997: *"The ward has never been so quiet."*

**Cradle & Co.** Toy maker of Harrow Falls. Tagline: *"The toy that's sensitive to your child."* Sign-off: *"Sleep tight. We're listening."* They sent "Listening Technicians" to collect Hushabies at night, and told families: *"If your Hushaby is no longer in your possession, contact us immediately. Please do not search for it yourself."*

## 4. The toys and the thing behind them

### The Hushaby
- **Listening Heart™**: learns the sounds of one child. *"When your child stirs, your Hushaby hums. When your child is quiet, your Hushaby listens."*
- **Adaptive Comfort Frame™**: *"Your Hushaby may seem larger over time."* They grow.
- **Settling**: it may hum on its own. This is "normal."
- Do not open the seams (inside is something like a warm, wet ear that turns toward you). Never wash it: it does not like water.
- Models HB-1 to HB-4. *"Not for children who sleep with the light on."*

### Mr. Hush (June's Hushaby)
A plush bear grown to about 2.6 m. Button eyes sewn shut with black thread, so **he is blind**. A sewn mouth that tears open into teeth. A split belly seam leaking stuffing and something wet. Arms too long, with sewing needles for claws. His head was sewn back on.
- He **hunts by sound**. He **hums a lullaby** while he wanders; **when the humming stops, he heard you.**
- He hears through walls, pipes and drains. Closed doors muffle him; they don't stop him.
- He learns your habits: hide in the same place twice and he checks there first.
- He can't hear through June's baby monitor. Nobody knows why.
- He isn't here for Diane. He never was.

### Thimble
Cradle's porcelain **rocking companion**, sold with the Hushaby: a boy doll in a sailor romper with a cracked face, glass eyes and arms too long. He lives in June's rocking chair.
- *"Thimble sleeps when your child sleeps. Thimble wakes when the house is quiet."*
- He **must be sung to sleep every night**: wind the music box and let it play to the end.
- If the song is missed he grows restless. **His shadow grows larger than he is**, and the rocking chair turns to face the bed.
- The music box plays **the same lullaby as the Hushaby**: *"They are meant to be together."* Which is why singing to Thimble brings Mr. Hush.

### The lullaby
The song is everywhere: Mr. Hush hums it, the music box plays it, the TV jingle sings over it, and June sings it "on the old lines". Diane says it is *her* song, one she made up for June that nobody else knows. Either the toys learned it by listening, or the song was never Diane's.

### MOTHER
Where the sounds go: "somewhere safe, somewhere warm." An experimental system under St. Agnes, part machine and part something grown, learning from children's voices. *"Mother doesn't want babies. She wants someone who can listen back."* It learns from crying. Its voice is stitched together from children's voices, and one of them is June's.

## 5. Chapter by chapter

### Prologue (built)
Text on black, then **June's tape**: *"Ellis? Is it recording? ... It's too loud in here. Everybody's breathing all the time. He says you're old enough now. He says you'll hear better than anyone. Come find me. Come home."* A low humming starts, and the tape cuts out.

### Chapter 1: The House (built)
*October 2011. The Marsh house, Harrow Falls. Night, rain, thunder.*

1. **The foyer.** The front door slams and locks behind Ellis. Every sound shows him the house for a moment.
2. **The living room.** Mr. Hush sits asleep in Mom's armchair, facing the TV static. Noise makes his head lift in his sleep.
3. **June's door** is locked, with Diane's note taped to it: *"If it's you, Ellis: the key is where I sleep. Walk softly. Don't run. It hears running."*
4. **Diane's bedroom.** The key is in her nightstand drawer. Taking it wakes him: lightning shows the empty armchair, and the humming begins.
5. **June's room.** The baby monitor is dead; Mom took the batteries out of everything. Ellis crosses the house, hunted, to the **kitchen** junk drawer for batteries, then back.
6. **The monitor.** June's voice comes through the static: *"Ellis...? He's coming up the hall. Don't let him in. Don't let him hear you. And sing to Thimble, Ellis. He gets big when it's quiet."* The door slams. The clock strikes three.
7. **The holdout, 3:00 to 6:00 AM.** Ellis sits on June's floor with the baby monitor (night-vision feeds of the hallway, living room and kitchen, and a talk-back that plays the lullaby in another room to lure him away). Thimble's chair has turned to face him. Mr. Hush tries the bedroom door, which Ellis has to fight shut, and taps at the window in June's voice (*"Let me in, Ellis."*). Meanwhile Thimble needs his song, and the song brings Mr. Hush. If anything gets in, it's over.
8. **Dawn.** The clock chimes six and grey light fills the room. Mr. Hush walks back to his chair. In the monitor's battery compartment is Halloway's last tape, **"Ellis"**: *"Your sister is alive. The house was only the ear. The rest of it is under St. Agnes. Go through the cellar. Follow the drain. Walk softly. And Ellis, I'm sorry. I'm the one who taught it your name."*

The note on the basement door already said where this goes: *"The cellar connects to the old storm drain. It goes all the way to the hospital. I heard children."*

### Chapter 2: St. Agnes Children's Hospital (planned)
*Through the cellar and down the old storm drain to the hospital where Hushabies were trial-tested at every bedside.*

- **Setting:** St. Agnes, flooded. The long-stay children's ward of the 1997 "Quiet Night" pilot, under Margaret Voss and Dr. Halloway.
- **Theme:** *The Listening Dark.*
- **New mechanics:** water. Every step ripples loudly and ripples in water linger. Floating objects drift and make noise on their own. Baby monitors let you listen to other rooms, but their static gives you away.
- **Creatures: the Ward Sisters.** Small nurse-model Hushabies, several at once. **They only move between your ripples**: each pulse shows them frozen, a little closer each time. Silence and darkness are the only way past them.
- **Lore to uncover:** patient charts, internal memos and parent complaints from the pilot; what happened to the ward's children; more of Halloway; the way down.
- **Holdout: the Nurses' Station.** The water is slowly rising. The Ward Sisters only move when your lights are off, and lights drain power.

### Chapter 3: Cradle Facility (planned)
*Underground, beneath St. Agnes, where MOTHER sleeps.*

- **Theme:** *Don't Wake It.*
- **New mechanics:** the **Pulse Device** sends a ripple on demand without moving, but drains a battery. **Cables** carry any noise made near them straight to MOTHER. An **alarm meter** shows MOTHER's wakefulness across the whole level; wake her fully and the facility hunts you.
- **Creatures:** **MOTHER**, huge and asleep, wired into everything through cables and fleshy roots. **The Echo**, which copies your sounds (footsteps, thrown stones, even your heartbeat) and makes fake ripples, so you can't trust what you see.
- **Holdout: MOTHER's Control Room.** A full power-management finale: keep the cables quiet, ration the Pulse Device, and watch the Echo fake sounds on your monitors.

### Chapter 4: Inside (planned finale)
*MOTHER's mind.*

- Surreal. **The rules flip**: something you learned to trust stops working. Your own ripples start lying to you.
- Every creature you've met appears together.
- The last challenge needs **the loudest sound in the game**.
- Either no holdout, or a twisted one where the room itself is a creature.

### Endings (planned)
- **Quiet ending:** Ellis escapes with June, but MOTHER is still listening.
- **Loud ending:** Ellis destroys MOTHER, and something is lost.
- **Secret ending:** unlocked by finding every tape and minigame. It reveals what MOTHER really is and what happened to Ellis as a child.

## 6. The big questions (for writers)

- **Why Ellis?** He "hears differently". Cradle screened for children like him and placed Hushabies in their homes on purpose. MOTHER wants someone who can listen back. Why it needed him *grown up* is for the later chapters.
- **Why the baby monitor?** It is the one thing in the house Mr. Hush can't hear through. Neither Diane nor Halloway knows why.
- **The lullaby** (see section 4): whose song is it?
- **"It only hums for me."** What exactly Ellis could hear as a child, and how Sensitivity's sound-sight (seeing by sound) connects to it, is the heart of the secret ending.
- **Halloway** taught it Ellis's name. Is he helping, atoning, or still working for Cradle?
- **What is June now?** "Alive, in whatever way a child can be alive down there", and one of MOTHER's voices.

## 7. Canon rules

- Place: **Harrow Falls**. Company: **Cradle & Co.** Product: **Hushaby** (a "Listening Companion" in hospital use).
- Dates: June vanished on **Nov 7, 1996** at about 3:00 AM. Chapter 1 takes place in **October 2011**. Ellis was 8 then and is 23 now; June was 6.
- Mr. Hush is **blind and hunts by sound**; humming means he's wandering, silence means he heard you.
- Thimble must be **sung to sleep**, and his song is the **same song** that calls Mr. Hush.
- Tone: FNAF-style creepy with some gore (stuffing, something wet, an ear inside the toy). Cheerful corporate voice on the surface, wrongness underneath.
- New documents go in `DOCS` / `TAPES` in `src/game/Lore.js` (and `LORE_ORDER`), and get a line in the Chapter's evidence table above.
